    • At any calculated risk: LDL-C 190 mg/dL or higher calls for a high-intensity statin; diabetes or CKD stage 3–4 at ages 40–75 calls for at least a moderate-intensity statin.
    • Not at goal on a statin: raise the intensity, or add ezetimibe or a PCSK9 inhibitor.
    • Measure lipoprotein(a) once in adulthood. 125 nmol/L (50 mg/dL) or more is a risk-enhancing factor, about 1.4 times the long-term risk; 250 nmol/L (100 mg/dL) or more, about twice. When elevated: earlier and more intensive LDL-C lowering and control of other risk factors. No Lp(a)-lowering drug is approved yet.
    • ApoB 120 mg/dL or more is a risk-enhancing factor. ApoB goals track non-HDL-C: under 90 mg/dL below 10% risk, under 70 at 10% or more. Measuring it is most useful with diabetes, CKM syndrome, triglycerides 150–499 mg/dL, or once the LDL-C goal is met, to find leftover risk.

    For everyone

    • Heart-healthy eating pattern and at least 150 minutes a week of moderate activity.
    • Current smokers: advise quitting at every visit and offer counseling plus medicine.
    • Overweight or obesity: support weight loss through diet, activity and, where appropriate, medicine.
    • Diabetes: an SGLT2 inhibitor or GLP-1 receptor agonist with proven cardiovascular benefit is worth considering.

    Summarized from the 2026 ACC/AHA multisociety dyslipidemia guideline, the 2025 AHA/ACC high blood pressure guideline and the 2019 ACC/AHA primary prevention guideline. A guide for discussion, not a substitute for clinical judgment. Check the full guidelines for details and recommendation classes.

    Use for adults 30–79 without known CVD. Not for people with known CVD, LVEF <40%, CAC ≥300, pathogenic inherited cardiovascular variants, end-stage kidney disease, or life expectancy under one year. 30-year estimates are given for ages 30–59.
